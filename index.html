02 · Envelope

One variable, three answers.

Lower glazing solar heat gain coefficient (SHGC) reduced energy cost by 7.5% and peak cooling load by 31.5%, while modelled emissions rose by 10.6%.

Changes relative to the control (SHGC 0.70). GHG = greenhouse gas. Results reflect this model’s climate, heating system and emissions factors.

03 · Ventilation

Ventilation changes the balance.

Across 30 → 10 cfm/person, peak cooling load changes little, while modelled emissions shift by 18 percentage points.

cfm/person = cubic feet per minute of outdoor air per person. Results for this model and climate; ventilation also has to meet indoor-air-quality requirements.

04 · Lighting

Lower lighting power. Higher heating demand.

A 25% reduction in lighting power reduced energy use intensity (EUI) by 6.3%, while heating demand rose by 13.6%.

Reduced lighting gains change the building’s heating and cooling needs.

05 · Measurement

Predicted. Not yet measured.

The model predicts a site EUI of 104.3 kBtu/ft²·yr. Testing that prediction requires measured building data.

方向 (01) Research direction

The simulations show trade-offs. My next step is to test how those predictions hold up in real buildings, using measurements to understand the effects of weather, controls and occupancy. I’m particularly interested in façades and natural ventilation in Hong Kong.
