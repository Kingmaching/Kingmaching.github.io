01 · Responsive shading

A façade that reacts to the sun

The skin of a building decides how much sun, heat and air get in. An adaptive façade changes that decision as the weather changes: closing against low afternoon sun, opening when the air outside is useful.

02 · Envelope

One variable, three answers

In ME 572 I lowered the glazing SHGC of a 5,000 ft² office from 0.70 to 0.30. Cooling and cost fell, but emissions rose: less solar gain shifted the load onto natural-gas heating.

03 · Air & systems

Outdoor air is a heating load

Cutting outdoor air from 30 to 10 cfm per person barely moved peak cooling in Rochester, but it changed emissions by about 18 points, all through heating. At WSP and Chevalier I see the other side: the ducts, pipes and pumps that carry these loads once they’re built.

04 · Lighting

Less light, more heat

My lighting layout hit 52 fc at 0.52 W/ft². In COMFEN, cutting lighting power by 25% lowered total EUI by 6.3%, but heating rose 13.6% because the lights were also heating the room.

05 · Measurement

Predicted, then measured

Every number above came from a simulation. Next, I want to instrument real buildings, measure them through changing weather and occupancy, and explain the gap, especially in hot, humid, high-rise cities like Hong Kong.

Direction

Why buildings

I grew up in Hong Kong, between dense high-rises and the mountains and coastline that are never far away. A visit to K11 Musea was the first time I noticed a building that tried to invite nature back in rather than shut it out.

Mechanical engineering gave me a way to turn that into engineering questions. Thermodynamics, fluid mechanics and building energy analysis changed what I see in a building: air-conditioning became heat transfer and loads, and a façade became a decision about solar gain, ventilation and comfort.

I’m now preparing for graduate study in building performance. I want to combine computational modelling with sensors and physical testing: predict how a system should behave, build or test it, measure what happens, and find out why reality differs.

Building energy 2026

Energy Simulation & Parametric Analysis

ME 572 Building Energy Analysis

  • Built a 5,000 ft² office energy model, benchmarked site energy against ENERGY STAR, and ran 17 parametric simulations of roof, wall, glazing and ventilation measures.
  • Lowering glazing SHGC from 0.70 to 0.30 cut energy cost by 7.5% and peak cooling by 31.5% but raised GHG emissions by 10.6%.
  • Found that one-at-a-time analysis isolates single effects but misses interactions between envelope variables.
